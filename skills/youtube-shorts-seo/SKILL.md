# YouTube Shorts SEO

Written from running a history Shorts channel (three a day at peak) and a
GTA 6 clips channel. The through-line: metadata wins the first test
audience, viewer behaviour decides everything after. Never over-invest in
metadata at the expense of the video itself.

## Before scripting: one target phrase

- Research search demand before scripting, not at upload: YouTube
  autocomplete, the Studio Analytics Research tab, Google Trends with the
  YouTube filter, or a keyword tool like vidIQ.
- Pick ONE target phrase per video. Cover a cluster of related queries
  across videos — do not use the same keyword in every video on a topic.
- Small channels: longer, specific phrases with moderate volume. Do not
  fight big channels for the head term.

## Titles

- Front-load the target keyword in the first 5 words and the first 50
  characters.
- 40–70 characters. Never all-caps, never blank, never clickbait the video
  does not deliver.
- Number-led titles work when the video genuinely delivers that structure
  ("5 Facts…", "3 Mistakes…") — in a 32,203-Short study they had the
  strongest matched-channel association (1.19x). "Why…" titles also
  outperform (1.08x). Yes/no question openers ("Can You…?") underperform
  (0.96x).
- Write three approaches (direct / question / number-led) and choose by
  the video, not by the formula ranking.
- Decide the title BEFORE production, not at upload.

## Descriptions

- First line: target keyword + a 1–2 sentence summary. This is the search
  snippet.
- Never blank, never one emoji, never the same description copy-pasted
  across Shorts. Opening sentences must be unique per video; only the
  footer may be reused.
- Structure: (1) keyword-rich opening, 2–3 sentences; (2) a link to a
  playlist or long-form video; (3) hashtags on their own line; (4) a
  standard reusable footer.
- Never keyword-stuff. Never put tags in the description — that is a spam
  policy violation.

## Hashtags and tags

- Hashtags: 3–5 per Short, in the description only, never in the title.
  Layer them: 1 broad (#Shorts), 1 category (#HistoryShorts / #GTA6),
  2–3 topic-specific. Never #viral #trending #fyp. More than 60 hashtags
  gets ALL of them ignored.
- Tags: 3–5 broad tags only — the exact topic phrase, common misspellings,
  brand/place names. Tags are a minimal ranking signal; do not spend
  production time on long tag lists. Keep total tag text under 500
  characters (YouTube silently truncates beyond it).

## Say it and show it

- Say the target phrase aloud in the opening seconds of the narration.
- Show it on screen too — the hook-text overlay should carry the title's
  keyword. YouTube's AI reads captions and on-screen text.

## Thumbnails

- Custom Shorts thumbnail: 9:16, 2160×3840 px, JPG/PNG, desktop Studio
  only, verified account. No Partner Program membership required
  (YouTube Help, Nov 2026). It shows on the channel page and in search;
  in the swipe feed it has ~0% influence — the first frame is the
  thumbnail there.
- Decide the title AND the thumbnail before production. Prepare at least
  2–3 clearly different thumbnails per Short.
- Design rules: max 3 elements (subject + text + background/accent); 2–4
  short words of bold high-contrast text; text complements the title,
  never repeats it; one clear focal point — the eye must know where to go
  instantly (3-second glance test at mobile size). Keep subject and text
  inside the central safe zone (~900×1160 px; ~380 px top/bottom reserved
  for UI overlays). Freeze the most dramatic moment mid-action.
- If a Short performs below your baseline, swap the thumbnail immediately
  — do not wait. Test & Compare does NOT work for Shorts; manual swap +
  compare over equal periods is the only method.
- Ask Studio can now suggest thumbnails from the video's transcript and
  themes (YouTube Help, Nov 2026) — check it in Studio.

## The related-video link

- Set the related-video link on EVERY Short (Studio → Content → the Short
  → Related Video). It is the only clickable link a Short has. Point it
  at the most relevant other Short or long-form video from the same
  channel.
- End screens and cards do NOT work on Shorts. URLs in Shorts descriptions
  and comments are NOT clickable. The burned-in end card and the
  related-video link are the only CTA mechanisms.
- Keep the spoken end-card CTA tight (~5s) — never lengthen it.

## Retention editing

- Speech or payoff within 2.5 seconds of the first frame. First sentence
  is a verdict, claim, question, or promise — never a greeting or setup.
- Something visible must change every 3–8 seconds (median ~5s). Any 8s+
  static stretch is a retention hole — cut it or change the shot.
- Open a loop in the first 5 seconds and hold the payoff until 55–85% of
  the clip — never reveal it early.
- Burned captions are mandatory on Shorts: 1–6 words per screen, one
  emphasis mechanism per video. Graphics in the top ~40% of frame,
  captions in the 48–66% band, never covering a face; two texts never
  share a zone.
- The first 7 seconds must confirm the packaging promise (title +
  thumbnail). Introduce a new curiosity loop within the first 30 seconds.
- End on the payoff, then stop.

## Diagnosing a stalled Short

- Before changing anything, check Viewed vs Swiped Away (Reach tab) and
  Average View Duration (Engagement tab) in Studio.
- Compare each Short against your own similar-length videos, not against
  other channels' viral hits. Read the retention graph for the exact
  drop-off point.
- Change ONE thing at a time and test it across several Shorts. One Short
  proves nothing.
- Do not delete a slow starter — Shorts can resurface weeks or months
  later. Edit metadata in place instead. Do not delete and repost — it
  risks a spam flag.
- Channel health metric: watch hours, not views. "Views are easy, watch
  hours is harder" (Derral Eves). Track watch-hour momentum over time.
- Viewer cycle: new → casual → regular takes ~6 months of consistent
  delivery. Design for the new viewer first; the regulars follow.

## Post-upload iteration

- Check the Reach tab at 7 and 28 days: which queries served impressions,
  what CTR each pulled.
- Every 60–90 days, refresh the descriptions of top-performing Shorts
  with current trending keywords.
- Low CTR but healthy impressions → new thumbnail, reworked title. Flat
  impressions → rewritten description.

## Channel-level SEO (set once, revisit rarely)

- Channel description (About): 1,000-character limit; primary keyword in
  the first 50 characters; first 100–150 characters are the search snippet.
- Channel keywords: Studio → Settings → Channel → Basic Info, 5–10 broad
  keywords. Low priority — set once.
- Upload defaults: Studio → Settings → Upload Defaults — default
  description footer, tags, language, category.
- Put every Short in a themed playlist.

## Patterns worth knowing (observed Nov 2026 — observe, never copy)

- Small channels break out on one Short (734 subs → 324K views; 98 subs →
  19.8K views). Size is not the gate; the video is.
- Numbered series and repeatable formats build return viewing. Make your
  series-ness visible in consistent packaging.
- The back catalog does the work: top channels get millions of monthly
  Shorts views from one recent hit plus older videos. This is why slow
  starters are never deleted and metadata gets refreshed.
- Both length extremes break out (6s and 180s). 60–90s history and
  ~30–70s gaming formats sit in the proven middle.
- Leaders post ~0.5–1 Short/day, not 3/day. Volume is not their edge —
  packaging is.

## Myths — do not apply these

- Test & Compare (A/B testing) does NOT work for Shorts — YouTube Help
  excludes them. Test across comparable uploads instead.
- There is no best time to post and no fixed test-batch size or 70%
  threshold — YouTube publishes none of these numbers.
- Hashtags do not rank a Short; tags are a minimal signal; file-name SEO
  is unconfirmed.
- The algorithm does not optimize for loops — a loop is a retention
  tactic, not a ranking hack.
- There is no shadowban; stalling is cold-start distribution, not a
  penalty.
- Custom Shorts thumbnails are NOT Partner-Program-only — YouTube Help
  requires only a verified account. (A vendor article claiming YPP-only
  is outdated/wrong.)
